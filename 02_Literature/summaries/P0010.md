# P0010 — Machine Learning Based Prediction Models for Cardiovascular Disease Risk Using Electronic Health Records Data: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## 2. Study Design & Cohort Characteristics
- **Setting & Country (Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis Scope):** PRISMA-guided systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis (`MedCalc`) searching PubMed/MEDLINE and Embase (plus backward reference search in ISI Web of Science) in March 2024 for English peer-reviewed primary-prevention EHR studies from January 1, 2010 to 2024 (pp. 3, 5, 15 [journal pp. 9, 11, 21]). Regional distribution across the `21` included studies (`22` dataset-country entries because Forrest et al. [59] used both US BioMe and UK Biobank, p. 5 & Table 4 p. 7): UK (`9` studies, `42.86%`; `41%` of datasets in Graphical Abstract p. 2), USA (`8` studies, `38.10%`; `36%` of datasets), Europe (`2` studies, `9.52%`; `9%`), China (`2` studies, `9.52%`; `9%`), and New Zealand (`1` study, `4.76%`; `5%`, p. 5, Fig. 2B on p. 11, Graphical Abstract p. 2). **South Asian / Bangladeshi representation:** **none / not reported** (`0` included studies from South Asia or Bangladesh; authors explicitly note *"most of the EHR sources are developed in countries and regions where the white ethnicity predominates"* and EHR availability *"remains limited in less developed areas"*, p. 13 [journal p. 19]).
- **Sample Size ($N$):** `1,551` records identified (`301` Medline, `1,146` Embase, `104` Web of Science) $\rightarrow$ `299` duplicates removed $\rightarrow$ `1,252` screened $\rightarrow$ `160` full-texts assessed (`139` excluded) $\rightarrow$ **`21` studies** included in systematic review (2016–2024) and **`20` studies** retained in meta-analysis (`1` study, Agrawal et al. 2021 [47, $N=13,782$], excluded to avoid UK Biobank double-counting with Kesar et al. 2022 [48, $N=464,547$], pp. 5–6, Fig. 1 on p. 6 [journal p. 12]). Meta-analysis initially extracted `45` ML and `35` conventional baseline models; after deduplicating overlapping cohorts (CPRD, UK Biobank, Vanderbilt), retained **`32` ML models** and **`26` conventional statistical models** (p. 1, p. 12 [journal pp. 7, 18]). Across the `21` primary cohorts, sample sizes averaged `470,965` patients (`SD 875,138.11`), ranging from `13,782` to `3,661,932` (p. 5, Table 4 on pp. 7–10).
- **Event Count & Prevalence:** Among the `21` included studies, `19` (`90.48%`) had unbalanced (`<30%`) CVD outcome distributions, with a mean event rate of **`7.48%` (`SD 0.06`)** (p. 6 [journal p. 12]; reported cohort event rates in Table 4 pp. 7–10 range from `1.16%` to `22.86%`, with `2` studies `NR`).
- **Endpoint Definition & Horizon:** Medium- to long-term (`5–15 years` eligibility criteria, Table 2 & text p. 4; actual included cohorts used `5` or `10` years, mean **`7.24` years [`SD 2.49`]**, p. 5) incident primary-prevention CVD risk prediction in asymptomatic adults ($\ge 18$ years) without prior CVD (pp. 1, 4, Table 2 on p. 4). Target CVD outcomes across included studies (p. 6, Table 4 pp. 7–10): CHD/IHD/CAD (`15` studies), stroke/TIA (`14` studies), MI/heart attack (`7` studies), heart failure, PAD, CVD death, and composite All-CVD. Inpatient/ICU (`MIMIC-III`), ED, post-surgical, AF-only, and single-modality ECG/imaging-only studies were excluded (pp. 4–5, Table 2 p. 4).

## 3. Modality & Technical Architecture
- **Modalities Included (Review Scope across 21 Studies):** Structured individual-level EHR (or EHR-linked biobanks like UK Biobank) (Table 2, p. 4). Across the `21` studies, feature counts averaged `109.62` (`SD 199.94`, range `5–826`; ~`30` excluding genetics/biomarkers, p. 10 [journal p. 16]). Included attribute categories (p. 10, Fig. 2F on p. 11):
  - **Clinical/EHR & Demographics:** Age (`21/21, 100%`), gender (`18, 85.71%`), ethnicity (`12, 57.14%`), SBP (`17, 80.95%`), BMI (`14, 66.67%`), diabetes (`17, 80.95%`), hypertension (`9, 42.86%`), kidney disease (`8, 38.10%`), family history of CVD (`8, 38.10%`), medications (`13, 61.90%`).
  - **Laboratory/Biomarkers:** Cholesterol (`18, 85.71%`), glucose (`8/21` approx. in Fig. 2F), additional biomarkers (`7 studies, 33.33%`, p. 10).
  - **Other Modalities:** Imaging (`3 studies, 14.29%`, e.g., CAC [65]), genetic features (`3 studies, 14.29%`), and `1` cross-modal transfer study (Jothi Prakash et al. 2024 [60], Table 4 p. 7) linking Vanderbilt EHR with PTB Diagnostic ECG Database and GEO gene expression data.
  - **Lifestyle:** Smoking (`13 studies, 61.90%`, p. 10); drinking, physical activity, and diet appear at low prevalence in Fig. 2F (p. 11; exact counts `not reported`).
- **ECG Format & Sampling:** `None` (systematic review of EHR models; studies using only ECGs/imaging/DNA without EHR were excluded, Table 2 p. 4; PTB ECG specs in [60] not detailed).
- **Missing-Modality / Missing-Data Handling (Reviewed Literature):** `17` (`80.95%`) studies reported missing-data handling; `11` (`52.38%`) used single (mean/median) or multiple (chained equation) imputation (p. 10).
- **Model Families & Baselines (Reviewed & Pooled):** `15` (`71.43%`) studies compared multiple ML models (`DL`: `12` [57.14%], best in `5` [23.81%]; `RF`: `9` [42.86%]; `GBM`: `6` [28.57%], best in `4` [19.05%]; `XGBoost`: `4` [19.05%], best in `3` [14.29%]; `LogitBoost`: `2` [9.52%]; other: `3` [14.29%], p. 6). `17` (`80.95%`) compared against conventional risk scores (`ASCVD/PCE` `8`, `QRISK` `5`, `Framingham` `4`, `Cox` `7`, `LR` `4`); `1` study [64] had no comparator (p. 6).
- **Fusion / Synthesis Strategy:** Not a primary model; **random-effects meta-analysis** (`MedCalc`) pooling C-statistic/AUROC across model families with $>3$ independent models (note: `NB`, `XGBoost`, and `QRISK` each have $n=3$ in Table 5 despite text stating *"more than three times"*, pp. 5, 12–13).

## 4. Quantitative Results & Evaluation Rigor
- **Data Split / Validation across Reviewed Studies:** Nearly all used $n$-fold CV and train/test splits; only `3/21` (`14.29%`) performed spatial external validation (`1` [4.76%] also temporal external validation); `10/21` (`47.62%`) shared code; `3/21` (`14.29%`) followed TRIPOD (p. 10 [journal p. 16]).
- **Pooled Discrimination & Heterogeneity (Random-Effects Meta-Analysis, Table 5 on p. 13 [journal p. 19], Fig. 4 on p. 14, text p. 12):**
  - **All ML (`n = 32`):** Pooled AUC **`0.815` (`95% CI: 0.787–0.842`, $P < 0.001$)**; $Q = 34,320.25$ ($P < 0.0001$), $I^2 = 99.91\%$ (`95% CI: 99.90–99.91%`).
  - **Conventional Risk Scores (`n = 19`, excluding LR & ASSIGN):** Pooled AUC **`0.765` (`95% CI: 0.734–0.796`, $P < 0.001$)**; $Q = 57,757.61$ ($P < 0.0001$), $I^2 = 99.97\%$ (`95% CI: 99.97–99.97%`).
  - **ML Subgroups (Table 5, p. 13):**
    - `Random Forest (RF, n = 6)`: **`0.865` (`95% CI: 0.812–0.917`)**, $I^2 = 99.70\%$ *(highest overall)*
    - `Deep Learning (DL, n = 6)`: **`0.847` (`95% CI: 0.766–0.927`)**, $I^2 = 99.93\%$
    - `GBM (n = 5)`: **`0.821` (`95% CI: 0.773–0.869`)**, $I^2 = 99.58\%$
    - `Ensemble (n = 19)`: **`0.820` (`95% CI: 0.790–0.850`)**, $I^2 = 99.88\%$
    - `Boosting (n = 11)`: **`0.796` (`95% CI: 0.763–0.829`)**, $I^2 = 99.92\%$
    - `XGBoost (n = 3)`: **`0.776` (`95% CI: 0.758–0.794`)**, $I^2 = 93.22\%$
    - `Naive Bayes (NB, n = 3)`: **`0.772` (`95% CI: 0.721–0.824`)**, $I^2 = 98.02\%$
  - **Baseline Subgroups (Table 5, p. 13):**
    - `Logistic Regression (LR, n = 7)`: **`0.796` (`95% CI: 0.750–0.842`)**, $I^2 = 99.95\%$
    - `Cox PH (n = 6)`: **`0.787` (`95% CI: 0.740–0.834`)**, $I^2 = 99.96\%$
    - `QRISK (n = 3)`: **`0.780` (`95% CI: 0.699–0.860`)**, $I^2 = 99.99\%$
    - `PCE (n = 5)`: **`0.760` (`95% CI: 0.721–0.798`)**, $I^2 = 99.54\%$
- **Publication Bias Tests (Table 5, p. 13; text p. 12):** Significant Egger's test publication bias detected in `Ensemble` ($-15.16$, $P = 0.0496$) and `Conventional` ($-38.32$, $P = 0.0096$); non-significant ($P > 0.05$) for all other subgroups and across all Begg's tests ($P > 0.05$).
- **Calibration Evaluated:** `No` quantitative synthesis possible (`7/21 = 33.33%` of included studies reported zero calibration; `6` used calibration plots, `4` calibration slope, `3` Hosmer-Lemeshow, `6` Brier score; pp. 4, 10, 14). *(Note: Abstract p. 1 claims *"While ML models show enhanced calibration"*, contradicting pp. 4 and 14 stating calibration could not be synthesized.)*
- **Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) / Clinical Impact:** `No` — `0` clinical utility, DCA, or cost-effectiveness impact studies found among included models (p. 13, 14).
- **Statistical Significance Testing:** All pooled subgroup AUCs differed significantly from $0.5$ ($P < 0.001$, Table 5 p. 13); formal pairwise statistical comparisons (e.g., meta-regression or $z$-tests between ML vs conventional AUCs) were not performed to explain $I^2 > 99\%$.

## 5. Validation Design & PROBAST-AI Risk of Bias Audit
- **Review-Reported PROBAST Audit of Included Studies (pp. 4, 10–12, Fig. 3 on p. 12 [journal p. 18]):**
  - **Domain 1 (Participants):** Mostly Low ROB (~85% Low, ~15% Unclear in Fig. 3, p. 12) due to strict primary-prevention EHR eligibility criteria.
  - **Domain 2 (Predictors):** `6` ML models (`13.0%`) rated **High ROB**; `4` models (`8.7%`, refs [59, 64]) rated **High applicability concern** due to excessive impractical biomarkers (pp. 11–12, Fig. 3).
  - **Domain 3 (Outcome):** Majority Low ROB (~91% Low, ~9% Unclear in Fig. 3; `1` model [`2.2%`, ref 59] Unclear applicability due to missing exact ICD codes, p. 11).
  - **Domain 4 (Analysis):** `13` ML models (`28.3%`) rated **High ROB** due to deficient reporting/handling of preprocessing, missing data, data complexity, and overfitting (p. 10, Fig. 3).
  - **Review's Overall PROBAST Summary:** **`16` ML models (`34.5%`) rated High Risk of Bias** (`41` models [`89.1%`] Low applicability concern, pp. 10–11, Fig. 3 on p. 12).
- **Overall PROBAST Risk of Bias (for `P0010` itself):** **Not Applicable (Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis)** — PROBAST applies to primary prediction model studies rather than meta-analyses; within the synthesized literature, the review documents **`34.5%` High ROB across included ML models** (p. 10). Review-level methodological caveats include omission of IEEE/Scopus/preprint databases, lack of meta-regression for extreme heterogeneity ($I^2 > 99\%$), unbundled supplementary PROBAST/extraction tables (`S1–S5`), and abstract/body mismatch regarding calibration claims (p. 1 vs pp. 4, 14).

## 7. Limitations & Relevance Critique
- **Author-Stated Limitations:** Extreme heterogeneity ($I^2 > 93\%–99.99\%$) across CVD ICD code definitions, feature sets, hyperparameters, and software packages; inability to pool calibration due to missing/inconsistent metrics; limited number of long-term primary-prevention EHR ML studies (`n=21`); predominance of Western/White cohorts (`UK/USA/Europe`); and complete absence of real-world clinical impact or cost-effectiveness evaluations (pp. 12–14 [journal pp. 18–20]).
- **Independent Reviewer Critique & FYDP Positioning:** Provides strong Q1 contextual benchmark evidence (`European Heart Journal - Digital Health`, 2025) supporting `PG1–PG5`: (1) confirms that EHR-only CVD risk models dominate primary prevention while excluding raw physiological waveforms (ECGs/imaging-only studies excluded, Table 2 p. 4) and omitting comprehensive lifestyle/diet/activity modalities (`PG1`, `PG2`, `PG4`); (2) proves that `33.3%` of CVD ML papers omit calibration, `85.7%` lack external validation, `85.7%` ignore TRIPOD, `34.5%` suffer High PROBAST ROB, and zero clinical impact/DCA studies exist; (3) documents severe ethnicity bias toward Western White populations (`0` South Asian/Bangladeshi studies, p. 13); and (4) exposes the near-total neglect of systematic XAI auditing in prior EHR CVD reviews (`PG3`, `PG5`). *(Metadata note vs `papers.csv`: `Access` is listed as `none`, whereas `P0010` is an Open Access CC-BY 4.0 article on p. 1.)*
