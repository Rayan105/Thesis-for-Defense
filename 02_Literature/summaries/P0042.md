## 2. Study Design & Cohort Characteristics
- **Setting & Country / Search Strategy:** PRISMA 2020-compliant systematic review (narrative synthesis only, no formal meta-analysis; pp. 1–3) searching **two biomedical databases** (`PubMed = 499`, `Cochrane Library = 1`) from inception plus reference-list screening and a **targeted supplementary evidence update through May 2026** adding `8` recent high-impact AI-ECG studies published after January 2023 (`[27–34]`, pp. 3–4, Fig. 1). Explicitly excluded engineering/informatics databases (`IEEE Xplore`, `Embase`, `Scopus`, `Web of Science`, pp. 3, 12).
- **South Asian / Bangladeshi Representation:** **none / not reported** in the main PDF (`14 pp.`). Study countries across all `108` studies reside in unbundled Supplementary Material (p. 3); representative Table 2 and Fig. 4 studies originate from Brazil (`CODE`), USA (`Mayo Clinic`, `UCSF`, `Yale`, `VITAL-AF`), China (`Zhu et al.`), Finland (`Santala et al.`), Germany (`Lueken et al.`, `Strodthoff & Strodthoff`, `Schwab et al.`), Taiwan (`Wu et al.`), Canada (`Kalmady et al.`), and UK (`Sau et al.`) (pp. 7–8, 13–14).
- **Sample Size ($N$) & Screening Funnel (pp. 4–5, 8, Fig. 1, Table 2):**
  - **PRISMA Funnel (Fig. 1, p. 4):** `500` database records identified (`PubMed n=499`, `Cochrane Library n=1`) $\rightarrow$ `12` duplicates removed $\rightarrow$ `488` screened $\rightarrow$ `229` excluded $\rightarrow$ `259` full-text assessed $\rightarrow$ `159` excluded (`76` ineligible design, `30` ineligible setting, `16` ineligible intervention, `11` not in English, `10` no full text, `7` wrong population, `6` outcomes not of interest, `3` irrelevant indication) $\rightarrow$ `100` from original search + `8` from targeted May 2026 update = **`108` included studies**.
  - **Included Cohort Sizes:** Range from small proof-of-concept cohorts (`159` patients, `Santala et al. 2021`; `268` patients, `Wu et al. 2019`; `297` patients, `Schwab et al. 2021`, Table 2 p. 8) to massive repositories (`2,470,424` ECGs from `1,773,689` patients in `CODE` [`Ribeiro 2019`]; `2,228,236` ECGs in `Sangha et al. 2022`; `180,112` ECGs from `70,692` patients in `Zhu et al. 2020`, p. 5 & Table 2 p. 8).
- **Event Count & Application Domain Breakdown (Fig. 2, p. 5):** Non-mutually-exclusive application counts across `108` studies: `Arrhythmia / AF` (`49`), `HF / LVEF / structural` (`22`), `Prognostic / risk` (`20`), `Wearable / mobile` (`17`), `MI / ACS` (`16`), `Image / multimodal` (`14`), `Signal quality / workflow` (`12`).
- **Endpoint Definition & Horizon:** Spans rhythm/conduction classification, prevalent and incident AF, MI/NSTEMI/OMI detection, reduced LVEF and structural heart disease phenotypes (`PAH`, `HCM`, `cardiac amyloid`, `MVP`), signal quality assessment, and long-term prognostic prediction (mortality, ASCVD, HF, sudden cardiac death; pp. 5, 8–10, Table 5).

## 3. Modality & Technical Architecture
- **Modalities Synthesized (Review Scope across 108 Studies, pp. 5, 8–9):** Primarily ECG across standard 12-lead, single-lead, Holter rhythm strips, wearable/mobile ECG, ECG images derived from 12-lead signals (`Sangha 2022`, `Schwab 2021`), and compressed/transformed signals. Several studies incorporate auxiliary/multimodal inputs: demographics, EHR features, clinical variables (`Wu 2019`), echocardiographic variables, coronary artery calcium (`Siva Kumar 2022`), and bioimpedance (pp. 5, 8–9, Table 2 & Table 4). **Lifestyle modality:** `none / not reported`.

  - **Other AI Computational / Workflow Systems:** **`8/108` (`7.4%`)** (clinical decision support, ECG language processing, annotation, workflow tools)
- **Synthesis Strategy:** **Qualitative narrative synthesis only** — formal meta-analysis was withheld due to extreme heterogeneity across populations, ECG formats, outcomes, thresholds, and validation strategies (pp. 1, 3, 8, 11).

## 4. Quantitative Results & Evaluation Rigor
- **Descriptive Representative Performance Ranges by Domain (Table 5 p. 10; Table 2 p. 8; Fig. 4 p. 7):** *(Explicitly presented as descriptive ranges across selected representative studies rather than pooled meta-analytic estimates, p. 8):*
  - **Arrhythmia & AF (`n = 14` representative):** Curated benchmarks often **`>0.950`** (up to **`~0.990`**) in accuracy/AUROC/Se/F1; clinically representative single-lead wearable (`Santala 2021`) achieved **`97.5%`** accuracy, **`100.0%`** sensitivity, **`95.4%`** specificity; handheld single-lead incident AF prediction (`VITAL-AF`, `Khurshid 2025`) yielded AUROC **`0.666–0.695`** (Table 2 p. 8, Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Multilabel ECG Classification (`n = 6` representative):** Mean AUROC **`0.970–0.990`**, mean F1 **`0.887–0.890`** (`Sangha 2022` internal AUROC `0.990`, external German `0.970`; `Zhu 2020` mean AUROC `0.983`, mean F1 `0.887`, Table 2 p. 8, Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Reduced LVEF Detection (`n = 3` representative):** AUROC **`0.918–0.930`** (`Attia 2019` retrospective `0.930`, prospective `0.918`), sensitivity/specificity mid-`80%` range (Table 2 p. 8, Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Structural Phenotype Recognition (`n = 7` representative):** AUROC **`0.770–0.960`** (`Tison 2019`: PAH `0.94`, HCM `0.91`, cardiac amyloid `0.86`, MVP `0.77`; plus phospholamban carrier status, HCM severity, `EchoNext` SHD screening, p. 7 & Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Myocardial Infarction & ACS (`n = 6` representative):** Selected clinical cohorts AUROC **`0.890–0.984`** (`Wu 2019` NSTEMI AUROC `0.984`, accuracy `92.9%`); benchmark Se/Sp **`>0.890`** (`Strodthoff & Strodthoff 2019` Se `93.3%`, Sp `89.7%`, Table 2 p. 8, Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Prognostic Modeling & Risk Prediction (`n = 10` representative):** More conservative estimates — incident AF AUROC **`0.666–0.838`**; ASCVD AUROC **`~0.800`**; mortality, HF, HTN-CVD, and SCD C-index/AUROC **`0.700–0.830`** (Table 5 p. 10).
  - **Signal Quality & Workflows (`n = 5` representative):** Signal-quality correlation `r = 0.750` validation / `0.600` testing (`Lueken 2023`, Table 2 p. 8); ED AF detection **`98.3%`** (`Schwab 2021`, Table 2 p. 8).
- **Calibration Evaluated (across 108 studies):** **Incomplete / largely omitted** — explicitly identified as a major weakness in the Analysis PROBAST domain (pp. 1, 7, 9 Table 3, 12).
- **Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) & Health Economics:** `No` — clinical utility and cost-effectiveness / health technology assessment are flagged as absent future priorities (pp. 1, 11–12).
- **Statistical Significance Testing:** `None` (no formal meta-analysis or pooled significance tests performed, p. 3).

## 5. Validation Design & PROBAST Risk of Bias Audit
- **Review's PROBAST Audit Across `108` Included Studies (Two Independent Reviewers + Consensus; p. 3, Fig. 3 p. 6, p. 7, Table 3 p. 9):**
  - **Domain 1 — Participants:** `Low / low-to-moderate`: **`9/108` (`8.3%`)** | `Moderate / moderate-to-high or not directly classifiable`: **`65/108` (`60.2%`)** | **High Risk: `34/108` (`31.5%`)** *(highly selected cohorts, single public benchmark datasets, poor real-world representativeness, Table 3 p. 9)*.
  - **Domain 2 — Predictors:** `Low / low-to-moderate`: **`52/108` (`48.1%`)** | `Moderate / moderate-to-high`: **`56/108` (`51.9%`)** | **High Risk: `0/108` (`0.0%`)** *(variable preprocessing, inconsistent predictor handling, opaque DL pipelines, Table 3 p. 9)*.
  - **Domain 3 — Outcome:** `Low / low-to-moderate`: **`86/108` (`79.6%`)** | `Moderate / moderate-to-high`: **`22/108` (`20.4%`)** | **High Risk: `0/108` (`0.0%`)** *(generally clear definitions, though variable adjudication/labeling consistency, Table 3 p. 9)*.
  - **Domain 4 — Analysis:** `Low / low-to-moderate`: **`13/108` (`12.0%`)** | `Moderate / moderate-to-high`: **`59/108` (`54.6%`)** | **High Risk: `36/108` (`33.3%`)** *(lack of external validation, overfitting risk, inadequate class imbalance handling, unclear train-test separation, missing calibration/robustness testing, Table 3 p. 9)*.
  - **Overall Risk of Bias (`n = 108`):** `Low / low-to-moderate`: **`12/108` (`11.1%`)** | `Moderate / moderate-to-high or not directly classifiable`: **`59/108` (`54.6%`)** | **High Risk: `37/108` (`34.3%`)** *(combined Moderate-to-High/High overall concern = **`96/108`, `88.9%`**!, Table 3 p. 9)*.
- **AMSTAR-2 / Methodological Critique of `P0042` Itself:**
  - *Strengths:* Large-scale (`108` studies) structured PROBAST appraisal using two independent reviewers + consensus tie-breaking (`p. 3`); clear exposure of the benchmark-vs-clinical performance gap (`Table 5 p. 10`).
  - *Limitations:* (1) **Restricted search (`PubMed + Cochrane` only, yielding `500` initial records)** omitting `IEEE Xplore`, `Embase`, `Scopus`, and `Web of Science`, plus a non-systematic targeted May 2026 update of `8` papers (pp. 3–4, 12); (2) no **PROSPERO** registration reported; (3) no inter-rater **Cohen's kappa** calculated (`p. 3`); (4) collapsed non-standard PROBAST reporting bins (`"low/low-to-moderate"`, `"moderate/moderate-to-high or not directly classifiable"`, `"high"`, p. 8 & Table 3) rather than strict `Low/High/Unclear` or `PROBAST-AI`; and (5) per-study PROBAST table (`Supplementary Table S1`) not bundled in the PDF.
- **Overall PROBAST Risk of Bias:** **Not Applicable (Systematic Review)** — Within its appraised corpus of `108` AI-ECG studies, **`34.3%` (`37/108`) are High Risk** and **`54.6%` (`59/108`) are Moderate/Moderate-to-High/Unclear** (only **`11.1%`** Low/low-to-moderate risk, Table 3 p. 9).

## 7. Limitations & Relevance Critique
- **Author-Stated Limitations (p. 12):** Substantial heterogeneity precluding quantitative pooling; dominance of retrospective public-benchmark and single-center designs inflating apparent performance; inconsistent reporting of preprocessing, class imbalance, calibration, and robustness; **search restricted to PubMed and Cochrane** (missing engineering/informatics venues); and non-systematic targeted May 2026 supplementary update.
- **Independent Reviewer Critique & FYDP Positioning:** Essential 2026 systematic review (`BMC Cardiovascular Disorders`) providing the largest formal PROBAST audit (`n = 108`) of AI-ECG literature: proves **`88.9%` (`96/108`)** of AI-ECG studies carry Moderate-to-High or High Risk of Bias (`34.3%` High overall; **`33.3%` High in Analysis**; **`31.5%` High in Participants**), directly caused by single public benchmark overuse, train-test leakage, class imbalance, missing external validation, and missing calibration (`Table 3 p. 9`). Also documents the sharp realism drop between curated arrhythmia benchmarks (`>0.95–0.99`) and real-world handheld/prognostic modeling (`AUROC 0.666–0.838`, `Table 5 p. 10`), validating our FYDP's rigorous patient-level splits, external validation, calibration/DCA, subgroup fairness, and waveform XAI faithfulness pipeline (`PG1–PG5`).
- **Discrepancies vs `papers.csv`:** Verified DOI (`10.1186/s12872-026-06234-5`), Year (`2026`), Authors, Title, `XAIUsed`, and `XAIEvaluated = No`. Minor notes in `papers.csv`: `Access` is `none` (whereas the PDF is CC-BY 4.0 Open Access, p. 1); `PROBASTRisk` is `Pending Step 007`; `SampleSize` says `See summary` (summarized above as `108` studies spanning `159` to `1,773,689` patients).
