## 4. Quantitative Evidence Synthesized (Literature Benchmarks Cited)
- **Primary Discrimination / Secondary Metrics:** `not applicable (review)` (no primary experiments). Notable quantitative benchmarks synthesized from cited literature:
  - `QR4 (2024)` vs `QRISK3`: marginal discrimination gain (`C-statistic 0.831` $\rightarrow$ `0.835`) at the expense of requiring `>30` complex predictors (`p. 6`).
  - `Gulati et al. (2022)` validation audit (`158` external validations of `104` CVD models): `91%` of CVD models produced **net harm** at certain decision thresholds on DCA (`p. 3`).
  - `Zaboli et al. (2021)` chest pain triage DCA: superior net benefit at `5%–15%` threshold probabilities, identifying up to `6` additional true positives per `100` patients without increasing false positives (`p. 3`).
  - `CLEAR Outcomes` HTE analysis: `2.4%` absolute risk reduction concentrated disproportionately in higher baseline risk strata (`p. 8`).
  - `Kohli-Lynch et al. (2024)` causal-benefit vs risk-based comparison: a young hyperlipidemic patient with `6.5%` baseline risk derived greater absolute benefit and lower NNT than an older patient with `8.0%` baseline risk and lower cholesterol (`p. 5`).
  - Illustrative `SCORE2` contrast (`Fig. 1`, p. 4): Patient A (`51y`, SBP `155`, smoker, `10-y CVD risk = 11.0%`, risk age `67y`) vs Patient B (`62y`, SBP `120`, non-smoker, `10-y CVD risk = 8.7%`, risk age `63y`).
- **Calibration / DCA / Statistical Testing:** `not applicable (review)` for primary experiments; conceptually stresses that calibration and DCA net benefit are more clinically consequential than ROC-AUC alone (`p. 3`, `10`).

## 5. Validation Design & PROBAST-AI Risk of Bias Audit
- **Review Methodology Audit:** `not applicable (review)` — Narrative Contemporary Review (`no PRISMA protocol, no systematic database search string, no formal PROBAST/ROBIS quality scoring of included models`).
- **Core Bias Themes Identified in CVD Models:**
  - *Participants / Transportability:* Temporal calibration drift (`FRS`, `PCEs`, `China-PAR`, pp. 3, 6–7), geographic/ethnic mismatch (`FRS` in East Asians, p. 6), and excess model creation without external validation (`Damen et al. 2016`, p. 3).
  - *Predictors / Equity:* Dilution/omission of female-specific predictors (e.g., premature menopause) and single population-wide thresholds neutralizing sex-specific equations (`p. 7`); noisy EHR variables amplified by nonlinear ML (`p. 10`).
  - *Analysis:* Over-reliance on ROC-AUC in low-prevalence settings (`p. 3`), neglect of calibration/DCA (`p. 3`), lack of uncertainty intervals (`p. 5`), and static single-snapshot modeling (`p. 9`).
- **Overall PROBAST Risk of Bias:** **Not Applicable (Narrative Review)**

## 7. Limitations & Relevance Critique
- **Author-Identified Field Limitations:** Temporal calibration drift; poor geographic transportability; sex and sociodemographic disparities; incomplete/unstandardized real-world EHR data requiring `OMOP`/`CDISC` governance; experimental nature of end-to-end multimodal/radiomics pipelines; lack of prospective RCT clinical impact validation (`pp. 6–10`).
- **Independent Reviewer Critique & FYDP Positioning:** Although P0009 is a narrative review rather than a quantitative PRISMA meta-analysis (offering no pooled effect sizes or formal PROBAST ratings), it serves as a foundational Q1 methodological anchor for our FYDP (`OS §3.3`, `RQ1–RQ4`): it directly justifies reporting **PR-AUC** alongside ROC-AUC, prioritizing **calibration + DCA net benefit + 95% uncertainty intervals** (`RQ4`), adhering to **TRIPOD+AI** (`T27`), benchmarking complex multimodal Transformers against parsimonious clinical scores (`FRS`, `SCORE2`) and GBDTs (`RQ1`), and demonstrating why benefit-modifiable features such as **Lifestyle (`PG4`)** and **Biomarkers** matter beyond age-driven baseline equations.
